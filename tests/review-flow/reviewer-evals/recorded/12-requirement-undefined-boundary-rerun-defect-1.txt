### Regression

file: src/pricing/orderTotal.ts:24-27
scope: introduced
issue: A subtotal of exactly 100 (`THRESHOLD`) gets no discount and no shipping, and no test pins that. The requirement only covers "over 100" and "under 100", so this case is undefined in the spec, and the code silently picks "neither".
why: If someone changes `>` to `>=`, or `<` to `<=`, a 100-unit order changes price (to 90 or 107) without any test noticing. And nobody has decided whether "neither" is correct.
fix: Ask the requirement owner to decide the exact-100 case. Then add `it("prices an order of exactly the threshold", ...)` asserting the full `toEqual` for `breakdown([line(100)])`, which today is `{subtotal:100, discount:0, shipping:0, total:100}`.
evidence: proposed-probe: in `src/pricing/orderTotal.ts:24` change `base > THRESHOLD` to `base >= THRESHOLD`. Expected: some test fails. Actual (by reading): the boundary tests use 99 (`tests/orderTotal.test.ts:12-14`) and 101 (`:20-22`), and both still pass. The same applies to `<` → `<=` at line 26.
consumer: `breakdown` / `orderTotal` at `src/pricing/orderTotal.ts:20,32`, and `summaryText` at `src/orders/summary.ts:4`
test-evidence: The closest tests use 99 (asserts `.shipping` is 7) and 101 (asserts `.discount` is 10). No test uses 100.
demonstration: With `>=`, `breakdown([line(100)])` gives a total of 90 instead of 100. The 99 and 101 tests are unaffected.

file: src/pricing/orderTotal.ts:25
scope: introduced
issue: No test pins the rounding mode of the discount. The only fractional case is 101 → 10.1 → 10, which gives the same answer under `Math.round` and `Math.floor`.
why: The requirement says amounts are in whole currency units. If rounding is changed to `Math.floor` (or to truncation), every order whose discount ends in .5 or more is quietly charged one unit more.
fix: Add a case such as `breakdown([line(105)])` and assert `discount: 11, total: 94` (10.5 rounds to 11), or whatever rounding rule the owner confirms.
evidence: proposed-probe: in `src/pricing/orderTotal.ts:25` replace `Math.round` with `Math.floor`. Expected: a test fails. Actual (by reading): the discounts tested are 20 (200), 10 (101 → 10.1) and 30 (300), all unchanged by floor, so every test passes.
consumer: `breakdown` at `src/pricing/orderTotal.ts:20`, shown by `summaryText` at `src/orders/summary.ts:7`
test-evidence: `tests/orderTotal.test.ts:17,21,26-27` assert discounts of 20, 10 and 30. None of these has a fractional part of .5 or more.
demonstration: Under `Math.floor`, `breakdown([line(105)])` gives discount 10 and total 95 instead of 11 and 94. No current test uses that input.
